Groundwork Takeoff vs Drawboard Projects · July 2026

Great markup.
But where's the takeoff?

Drawboard Projects is genuinely good design-review software — best-in-class pen inking, automatic revision overlays, honest low pricing. It measures exactly two things: calibrated lengths and simple areas. No counts, no volumes, no assemblies, no pricing, no bid. That's not a flaw — it's a different category. Here's where the line is.

TL;DR: If your team only reviews drawings — comments, revisions, issue tracking — Drawboard Projects is honest value at roughly $270/seat/yr, and we won't pretend otherwise. The moment you have to count, price, or bid, it has nothing for you: measurement stops at length and simple area, and the only export is a task list. Groundwork Takeoff does the full job — measure, assemble, price, export — in one $950/yr seat.

When Drawboard wins

We'll say it plainly.

If your team's whole job is design review — architects and engineers redlining drawings with a pen, tracking issues, comparing revisions, pushing tasks into Procore or Teams — Drawboard Projects is excellent at that, and at roughly $270 a seat per year it's honestly priced. The inking alone is the best in the industry. This is not a hit piece; it's a category distinction.

But a contractor's day doesn't end at review. You have to measure six ways, count fixtures, apply assemblies, price the job, and export the bid. Drawboard has no path from a drawing to a number. Groundwork does the whole chain in one seat — takeoff, live estimate grid, Excel/CSV export, full markup with a Revision Log, and plan-compare overlay included — so you're not paying for two tools and re-keying data between them.
