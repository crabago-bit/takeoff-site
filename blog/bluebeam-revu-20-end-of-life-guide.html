Bluebeam Revu 20 end-of-life:
your complete migration guide

Support ended July 31. On December 31, Revu 20 loses Studio, updates, and everything else. Here's what's actually happening, what each path costs, and how to move in 30 minutes.

What's actually happening

If your company bought Bluebeam Revu 20 perpetual licenses — the last version Bluebeam ever sold outright — two dates matter this year. July 31, 2026 was End of Support: no more updates, patches, or technical support. December 31, 2026 is End of Life: Studio Sessions and Studio Projects stop working, and license transfers are disabled.

That second date is the one that bites. Studio is how most Revu shops share sessions with GCs and reviewers. When it goes dark, a "perpetual" license becomes a stand-alone PDF editor that can never move to a new machine.

Bluebeam stopped selling perpetual licenses in September 2023. There is no path to keep owning Revu — the only Bluebeam option is a subscription.

What to check before you switch anything

  • Your markup archive. Export or flatten critical marked-up sets from Studio Projects before December 31 — after EOL you can't pull them from Studio.
  • Tool sets and profiles. Screenshot or document your custom tool chest items; every replacement tool will need them rebuilt once.
  • What you actually use. Most estimating teams use a fraction of Revu: measure, count, markup, overlay, export. If your list looks like that, you don't need a document-management platform — you need a takeoff tool.
  • Scripting and batch Sets. If your workflow genuinely depends on Revu's scripting engine or batch Sets processing, a Bluebeam subscription is the honest answer. Few takeoff-focused teams are in this bucket.

The 30-minute migration (to Groundwork Takeoff)

  1. Minutes 0–5: Start a trial (no card) and open a real plan set from a live bid. PDFs are PDFs — there's nothing to convert.
  2. Minutes 5–10: Verify the scale (auto-detected; confirm once per sheet) and trace one wall with the Linear tool. Keyboard: L for Linear, A for Area, C for Count.
